# Mid-Century Modern
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
Mid-Century Modern describes design roughly from the 1940s through the 1960s, closely tied to postwar optimism, new manufacturing techniques, and the rise of suburban American life. It grew out of the broader Modernist belief that form should follow function, but softened that rigor with warmth, organic curves, and natural materials, aiming to make modern design feel livable rather than purely industrial.

## How do you recognize or use it?
Characteristics: clean lines mixed with organic, tapered forms, a mix of natural materials (wood, leather) with new industrial ones (molded plastic, metal), functional minimalism without being cold, and a muted but warm color palette (mustard, teal, walnut brown, burnt orange).

It suits brands wanting to signal approachable modernity, craftsmanship, and timeless design over trend-chasing — furniture, home goods, and lifestyle brands use it often.
