# Unity
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
Unity is one of Robert Cialdini's principles of persuasion, introduced in his book *Pre-Suasion* as an extension of his original six. It argues that people are most persuaded not just by liking someone, but by sharing an identity with them — a "we" rather than a "you and I." When a brand frames the audience as already part of its group, agreement feels less like being sold to and more like acting in line with who you already are.

## How do you recognize or use it?
Practical uses: language that frames the brand and audience as one group ("we," "our," "join us" rather than "you should buy"), shared identity markers (community, cause, or in-group language), and user-generated or community content that shows the audience as co-creators rather than customers.

It differs from social proof, which shows that other people approve; Unity goes further by implying the audience and the brand are the same people, making the request feel like self-consistency rather than persuasion from outside.

## Examples
Application: a software company's landing page headline reads "Built by [profession], for [profession]" instead of "The best tool for [profession]." The CTA reads "Join us" rather than "Sign up."

This works because it frames the company as part of the same in-group as the visitor, not an outside vendor making a pitch. The ask feels like joining people like you, not responding to a sales message.
