# Sage

## What is it?
The Sage is one of the twelve brand archetypes described by Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson in *The Hero and the Outlaw*. It belongs to the group organized around independence, alongside the Innocent and the Explorer. Its promise is that the truth will set you free, and that understanding the world matters more than conquering or belonging to it.

Core traits:
- Desire: to find the truth
- Goal: to use intelligence and analysis to understand the world
- Fear: being deceived, misled, or ignorant
- Strategy: seek out information and knowledge, self-reflection, understanding thought processes
- Gift: wisdom, intelligence
- Trap: can become too in their head, paralysis by analysis, "detached, boring intellectualism"

## How do you recognize or use it?
Audience: people who value expertise, research, and evidence over hype or emotion. It suits education, consulting, research institutions, publishing, and any brand positioning itself as a trusted source of truth rather than a seller of goods.

Sage design favors clarity: clean typography, generous white space, restrained color (often muted blues, grays, or a single academic accent). Copy is precise and evidence-based, often citing sources directly rather than making unsupported claims. Imagery tends toward diagrams, books, archives, or quiet study spaces over lifestyle photography.

## Examples

### Example 1
![Describe the design](../assets/heroes/sage/modernist.png)

Archetype: Sage
Style: Swiss/International Style
Persuasion: Authority
Headline: "Know before you decide."
CTA: "Read the research" — invites verification, not impulse

A grid-based, typographic layout with restrained color and no decorative imagery signals rigor and clarity. The CTA leans on authority: the brand doesn't ask to be trusted, it offers the evidence directly.

### Example 2
![Describe the design](../assets/heroes/sage/postmodernist.png)

Archetype: Sage
Style: Deconstructivism
Persuasion: Reciprocity
Headline: "We'll teach you first."
CTA: "Get the free guide"

A fragmented, layered postmodern layout (overlapping text, intentionally broken grid) mirrors how real understanding is built piece by piece rather than handed down cleanly. The CTA uses reciprocity: offering real knowledge for free before asking for anything builds trust with a Sage audience, who are suspicious of brands that lead with a sales pitch.
