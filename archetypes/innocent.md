# Innocent
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
The Innocent is one of the twelve brand archetypes described by Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson in *The Hero and the Outlaw*. It belongs to the group organized around independence and fulfillment, alongside the Explorer and the Sage. Its core promise is simplicity: the world can be good, honest, and safe if you choose to see it that way.

Core traits:
- Desire: to be happy, safe, and good
- Goal: to be happy
- Fear: doing something wrong, being punished for it
- Strategy: do things right, stay optimistic
- Gift: faith and optimism
- Trap: denial, repressing problems rather than facing them

## How do you recognize or use it?
Audience: people drawn to simplicity, nostalgia, and trust over complexity or edge. It suits products tied to purity, wholesomeness, family, or a return to basics.

Visually, Innocent design favors light, open space, soft or pastel color, rounded shapes, and plain, honest language. Photography is warm and natural, rarely stylized or ironic. Copy avoids jargon and cleverness in favor of directness, since the archetype's whole claim is that nothing is being hidden.

It fits brands selling comfort food, baby and family products, natural or "clean" goods, and anything positioning itself as a relief from an overcomplicated world.

## Examples

### Example 1
![Describe the design](../assets/heroes/innocent/modernist.png)

Archetype: Innocent
Style: Mid-Century Modern
Persuasion: Liking
Headline: "Simple, the way it should be."
CTA: "Try it today" — low-commitment, inviting action

A clean, uncluttered hero banner using a restrained mid-century palette (cream, soft yellow, muted sage) and plain geometric shapes communicates simplicity and warmth without ornament. The headline states the Innocent's core promise directly, and the soft, approachable tone builds liking rather than urgency.

### Example 2
![Describe the design](../assets/heroes/innocent/postmodernist.png)

Archetype: Innocent
Style: Vaporwave
Persuasion: Social proof
Headline: "Back to simple."
CTA: "Join thousands who switched back"

A softened, nostalgic postmodern treatment (pastel gradient, light grain, retro type) frames the brand as a return to an idealized, simpler past. The CTA leans on social proof, since an Innocent audience trusts that many others already made the safe choice.
